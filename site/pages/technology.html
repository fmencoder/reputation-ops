How the layers converge

The four areas above are usually discussed separately. In a working system they are one path. Data and events arrive, an intelligence layer interprets them, autonomous agents decide what to do, and a policy and authorization layer decides whether the agent is permitted to do it. Only past that gate do actions reach smart contracts, distributed state, and the financial and digital infrastructure underneath — and what comes out the far end has to be a verified outcome, not merely a completed call.

The gate is the part that gets left out. An agent that can reason about a transaction and an agent that is authorised to execute one are different systems, and collapsing them is how autonomy becomes an incident. Everything between the intelligence layer and the infrastructure layer sits inside a containment boundary: security and governance on one edge, observability and human oversight on the other. Those are not adjacent concerns bolted on afterwards; they are the walls that make the middle of the diagram safe to run.
