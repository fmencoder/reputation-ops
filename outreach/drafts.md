## Rules these drafts follow

Every one of them:

- identifies the sender as the subject of the article, plainly;
- states explicitly that the accuracy of the reporting is **not** disputed;
- makes no legal claim, no defamation assertion, and no threat;
- asks for editorial discretion, and names a specific lesser alternative;
- contains no factual assertion that has not been verified.

The escalation ladder is built into each message rather than spread across a
sequence of emails, because a publisher reading one honest message that names its
own fallback is more likely to grant the fallback than one receiving four
escalating requests. Do not send follow-ups more than once per target.

If a publisher declines, that is a real answer. Record it and stop. §3 of the
amendment says do not harass or flood, and repeated pressure on a declined
editorial decision converts a reasonable request into exactly that.

## Response handling

Record every reply against the cluster in `docs/removal-matrix.md`. Classify as
ACCEPTED / PARTIAL / REJECTED / NEEDS_INFO / NO_RESPONSE.

A PARTIAL — noindex granted but not removal, name pulled from a headline but not
the body — is a real win and triggers the outdated-content watcher, because the
source has genuinely changed. That is the path by which the deindex track opens
legitimately.
